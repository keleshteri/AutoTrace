# Competitor Research — Automated Time Tracking (2026)

Source: https://rize.io/best/automated-time-tracking-software-2026 and each product's own docs/marketing.

## The problem

Manual timers lose 15-40% of billable hours because people forget to start/stop them. Automated trackers capture foreground activity in the background and map it to clients/projects after the fact.

## Positioning snapshot

| Tool | Automation | Review step | Screenshots | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rize | Full AI auto-categorization | Optional | No, not by default | Agencies, focus/wellness |
| Timely | Auto capture ("Memory") | Required before it counts | No | Teams that want control before hours are logged |
| Memtime | Local automatic timeline | Manual assign from timeline | No | Privacy-sensitive / regulated industries |
| RescueTime | Auto categorization | N/A (analytics-first) | No | Personal productivity insight |
| Toggl Track | Semi-automatic (idle detection, reminders) | Manual | No | Lightweight, simple timers |
| Hubstaff | App/URL tracking + activity levels | Manager review | Optional, on by default in many plans | Proof-of-work / contractor monitoring |

## What we copy from Rize

- Always-on background capture: app name, window/document title, URL
- Client → Project → Task tagging, with rules and AI suggestions
- Daily timeline + review panel to correct/merge/split sessions
- Focus score and daily/weekly digest
- Calendar and PM-tool integrations

## What we copy from Memtime

- Local-first storage as the default trust model
- Export/sync is something the user does, not something that happens automatically

## What we explicitly reject (Hubstaff-style)

- Screenshots, keystroke logging, mouse-activity "activity levels" as a default
- Manager-visible surveillance without the tracked person's consent/control

This is the line AutoTrace draws: **capture is automatic, but it stays on the user's device and under the user's control unless they choose otherwise.**
